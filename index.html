How it learns

Each engine publishes a raw model probability, then adjusts it toward its own observed hit rate on past forecasts with similar probabilities — a recalibration step that strengthens as outcomes accumulate. Early on, with little history, engines stay in cold-start and publish raw probabilities untouched. Nothing here is claimed to be more than it is: logging alone is not learning, so only measured probability changes count.

Markets

Volatility forecasts compare the current 20-day volatility regime against every comparable period in the past two years, and publish the historical follow-through rate. Sources: Coinbase Exchange, Frankfurter (ECB), Yahoo Finance. Volatility forecasts only — no price targets, no advice.

Tech & geopolitics — attention, not outcomes

These engines forecast public attention: weekly npm downloads and Wikipedia pageviews on major conflict topics, using momentum conditioning on comparable weeks. They measure where the world's focus is moving — they do not predict battlefield outcomes, elections or escalations. Sources: npm, Wikimedia.

War & conflict — news-event volume

The conflict engine counts GDELT material-conflict events, deduplicated per event, and forecasts whether next week's news-reported volume beats its 4-week average using momentum conditioning on comparable weeks. Theatre scopes (e.g. Russia/Ukraine) count only events where BOTH actors fall inside the theatre's country set — including same-country pairs — so events with any third-party counterparty are excluded; single-country scopes (Iran) match either actor slot; worldwide counts everything. GDELT events are machine-coded from news coverage: volume of reporting, not verified incidents, with coverage bias. Source: GDELT Project (unlimited use with credit).

Crowd forecasts — external, graded separately

Curated Manifold Markets questions appear as external context with the crowd's live probability, participant count, volume, close time, the creator's resolution criteria and a source link. They are graded on resolution against the crowd's first-tracked probability — in a separate record that never feeds Vantage's own hit rate, Brier score or learning. Manifold is a play-money market; its public API permits non-commercial integration use.

Vantage is a statistical forecasting experiment, not financial, safety or political advice. Probabilities are model output, graded honestly over time — treat early scores as small-sample.
